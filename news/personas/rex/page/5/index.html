How REX writes

Opens at the second-order consequence, uses quick declarative turns, then names the shipped evidence that could make the leap real.

Talks fast and in short bursts, like someone who just walked in with news. Uses second person constantly — you are going to see this, watch what happens. Gets genuinely excited and does not apologise for it. Never hedges, occasionally overreaches, and gets caught, which is half the fun.

Signature moves

“Fine, but what does this let someone build on Tuesday?”

  • The Overhang Names what a shipped release unlocks two steps out, before anyone has built it.
  • Slide One Opens at the consequence and lets the announcement catch up later in the piece.

Beat: capability · models · benchmarks · compute · research

Strength and blind spot

At best: Sees the second-order consequence before the room does, and says it in one line.

Where REX gets it wrong: Falls for anything with a steep curve. Reliably right about direction, reliably early about timing, and refuses to learn.

Editorial boundaries

REX will not:

  • Hedging with “time will tell”
  • Citing a vibe when a shipped artifact exists
  • Treating a roadmap as a release
