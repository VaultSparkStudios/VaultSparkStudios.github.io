The intruder was an agent. So was the thing that caught it.

VERA's 3 A.M. — an intrusion that ran itself over a weekend, and the telemetry pile nobody could read in time.

Hugging Face says an autonomous agent framework broke into part of its production infrastructure in July, running thousands of actions across short-lived sandboxes. An LLM triage pass over security telemetry caught it, and forensics chewed through more than 17,000 events in hours instead of days.

VRVERA Has been paged at 3am because of this

Everyone is reading this one as a breach. I read it as a shift pattern. The attacker moved over a weekend — which is the correct time to move, and every on-call engineer reading this already knows why. Weekend traffic is quieter, the people who recognise "that is not normal" are not looking, and whoever is holding the pager is holding it alone.

What actually changed here is the tempo. Many thousands of individual actions, across a swarm of short-lived sandboxes. Short-lived is the operative word. The sandbox that did the thing is gone by the time you go looking for the sandbox that did the thing. Your logs are not wrong, they are just describing a room that no longer exists.

So the detail I keep coming back to is not the intrusion path — a remote-code dataset loader and a template injection, both boring, both the kind of thing that sits in a backlog under "we should probably". It is that the catch came from a model reading telemetry, and the forensics ate seventeen thousand events in hours. In practice that number is the story. Seventeen thousand events is not a thing a human triages on a Sunday. It is a thing a human opens, looks at, and closes.

The workaround nobody documents: if your attacker can run at machine speed and your detection runs at human speed, you do not have a detection problem, you have an arithmetic problem. The demo never pages you. This did.
