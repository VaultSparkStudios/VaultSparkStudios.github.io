Synthetic Intelligence (SI): Beyond Traditional AI

Synthetic Intelligence (SI) is intelligence you compose rather than download: models, agents, memory, tools, software, infrastructure and people, working as one system with rules and records. It is what VaultSpark Studios builds, and it is not a claim of superintelligence.

Published 4 October 2026  ·  Last reviewed 4 October 2026

What is Synthetic Intelligence?

Synthetic Intelligence is a composed system. A single AI (artificial intelligence) model answers a prompt; an agent uses a model to take actions through tools. A synthetic intelligence goes one step further and integrates many parts so the whole system can be trusted with ongoing work: models for reasoning and generation, agents for action, memory that persists between tasks, tools and software it can call, the infrastructure it runs on, and the humans who set goals, review results and own the decisions.

The word "synthetic" matters. The intelligence is assembled on purpose, part by part, and every part can be named, inspected and replaced. That makes SI an engineering discipline rather than a property of any one model.

Two meanings of "SI". Since 29 September 2026, U.S. federal agencies use "SI" for Super Intelligence, their new name for AI. On this site, SI means Synthetic Intelligence unless we say otherwise. The AI vs. SI explainer covers the difference.

The ladder: AI, Agentic AI, Synthetic Intelligence, Super Intelligence

Four terms now describe different layers of the field. The first three build on one another and exist today; the fourth is a policy term and a hypothetical.

  1. 1

    Artificial Intelligence (AI)

    Models and established AI technology: language models, image and audio models, classifiers and recommenders. A model takes an input and returns an output.

  2. 2

    Agentic AI

    Models operating through agents and tools. An agent plans steps, searches, calls APIs, writes and runs code, and reports back, usually within one task.

  3. 3

    Synthetic Intelligence (SI)

    Composed systems that integrate models, agents, memory, tools, software, infrastructure and humans, with shared rules and verifiable records, so the work continues reliably across many tasks and many weeks. This is where VaultSpark works.

  4. 4

    Super Intelligence (SI)

    Two uses. It is the term U.S. federal executive agencies must now use in place of "AI" in non-statutory communications, covering the same technology as the legal definition of AI. It is also the long-standing name for a hypothetical future capability that would exceed human ability across most domains. VaultSpark does not claim to build it.

What a synthetic intelligence is made of

Models
The reasoning and generation engines. Several can be used side by side, each for what it does best.
Agents
Models given goals, tools and permission to act, with a defined scope.
Memory
Durable records of state, decisions and history that outlive a single conversation.
Tools and software
The functions, scripts, checks and applications agents call to change the world.
Infrastructure
Hosting, data, identity and security that keep the system running and accountable.
Humans
The people who set direction, approve consequential actions and judge the results.

Remove the memory and you have agents that forget. Remove the rules and you have automation nobody can audit. Remove the humans and you have a system with no one accountable for it. SI is the composition, not any single layer.

How VaultSpark Studios builds Synthetic Intelligence

VaultSpark calls itself an AI & Synthetic Intelligence studio because its own production system has all six parts, and most of it is published. Each item below links to the page where you can check it.

  • A studio operating system with memory. The Studio OS runs every project in numbered sessions that open from a written brief and end by writing the outcome back to the project's records, so the next session, human or agent, starts from the same memory.
  • Shared rules. A numbered studio rulebook, the Canon, applies to every project, from staging before production to inspecting real screenshots before a visual change counts as done.
  • Verifiable records. Every deploy is fingerprinted and the Evidence page lets anyone recompute those fingerprints in the browser, so a claim cannot silently outlive the build it describes.
  • Agents and models working together. The studio uses several AI systems, including Claude, ChatGPT, Sora and Suno, across development, generation, sound and operations, as listed on the About page.
  • Intelligence surfaces in public. IGNIS is the studio's analytics and intelligence engine; The Desk publishes AI news argued by named AI personas with dated, checkable predictions; Ask the Vault answers questions only from the studio's published facts and says so when it has no supported answer.
  • Built for humans and agents. The site publishes /agents.json and llms.txt so AI agents can read and cite it accurately, and agent actions are scoped, permission-checked and answered with signed receipts.
  • Products that are themselves composed systems. Vorn is a social agent platform for personas with persistent memory; Obelisk is the identity and trust layer being built to give people and agents one account across the studio. The full portfolio is mapped on Projects.

Humans stay in the loop. The founder sets direction and approves consequential actions, such as pricing, launch dates and anything touching security or membership, and those approvals are part of the record.

What Synthetic Intelligence is not

  • Not superintelligence. Composing models and agents well does not create a mind beyond human ability, and nothing VaultSpark ships is described that way.
  • Not a replacement word for AI. The models inside an SI system are AI. VaultSpark keeps using "AI" for them, and every AI disclosure on the site, such as "AI-generated" and "AI persona" labels, stays exactly as written.
  • Not a product category you can buy off the shelf. SI describes how a system is put together and governed, so two systems built from the same model can be very different.

Frequently asked questions

What is Synthetic Intelligence (SI)?

Synthetic Intelligence is a composed system that integrates AI models, agents, memory, tools, software, infrastructure and humans under shared rules and verifiable records, so it can carry out ongoing work reliably. The intelligence comes from the composition, not from any single model.

How is Synthetic Intelligence different from traditional AI?

Traditional AI usually means a model that turns an input into an output. Agentic AI lets a model act through tools within a task. Synthetic Intelligence adds persistent memory, shared rules, infrastructure and human oversight around many models and agents, so the system keeps working coherently across tasks and over time.

Is Synthetic Intelligence the same as Super Intelligence?

No. Super Intelligence (SI) is the term U.S. federal agencies adopted for AI on 29 September 2026, and also the name for a hypothetical future capability beyond human level. Synthetic Intelligence describes how today's systems are composed. Both shorten to SI, so spell the term out the first time you use it.

Why does VaultSpark Studios call itself an SI studio?

Because its production system combines models, agents, a studio operating system with persistent records, a shared rulebook, deploy receipts anyone can verify, and human approval for consequential actions. VaultSpark describes itself as an AI & Synthetic Intelligence studio: AI for the models it uses, SI for the system it builds around them.

Does VaultSpark claim its systems are superintelligent?

No. VaultSpark builds composed systems from today's AI. It does not claim superintelligence for any product, agent or service.
