How We Build

A reader's guide to the studio's working record

Why this page exists

VaultSpark Studios publishes its own working record. The Status, IGNIS, Oracle, and Roadmap pages are fed by the same notes, scores, and rules the studio uses to run itself, so they carry some words that were never written for visitors: session numbers, a score out of 1000, rules with three-digit labels, and the odd "closeout" or "handoff". This page explains what each of those means, in plain language, so the record can be read by anyone who finds it. Where a term appears elsewhere on the site, it links back here.

Sessions

The studio works in numbered sessions: S1 was the first, and the count keeps climbing. A session is one bounded block of work on one project. It opens with a short brief that restates the project's current state and the goal for the day, and it ends with a closeout that writes the outcome back into the project's records: what shipped, what was decided, and what comes next. That is why a session number often appears next to an entry in the Changelog or a post in the Signal Log. The number is a timestamp with a memory attached.

The SIL score

SIL stands for Studio Intelligence Ledger. At the end of every session the studio grades its own work across ten categories, each scored out of 100, for a total out of 1000. The categories cover things like whether the work shipped, whether the records were kept current, whether anything was left half-done, and whether the session found a way to make the next one easier.

Two honest caveats. The SIL score is a self-assessment, not an external benchmark: nobody outside the studio grades it, and it is not comparable to any other studio's number. It is published because a trend line the studio cannot quietly edit is more useful than a private one. The current score and its trend live on the IGNIS page.

Canon

Canon is the studio's numbered rulebook. Each rule has a label such as CANON-007 and applies to every VaultSpark project, so when a page cites one it is naming the rule it is being held to. A few that surface on the public site:

  • CANON-006 · Branding link-back — every public VaultSpark product carries VaultSpark Studios branding that links back to this site.
  • CANON-007 · Staging before production — every project deploys to a staging copy first, and the live site is only updated after that copy checks out.
  • CANON-031 · Observability honesty — a dashboard may only show a signal it actually measured; when data is missing it says so instead of guessing.
  • CANON-053 · Rendered-pixel checks — a visual change is not finished until a real browser screenshot of it, on desktop and mobile, has been inspected.

Rules are cited most often on the Status page, where several tiles name the rule they are enforcing.

Receipts and proof

A receipt is a small published record that ties a claim to a specific build. Every deploy of this site is hash-bound: the files that make it up are fingerprinted, and the fingerprints are published alongside the pages. When a page says a check passed, the receipt names the exact build that check ran against, so a passing mark cannot silently outlive the code it graded.

Receipts are meant to be re-checked by people who do not take the studio's word for it. The Evidence page's in-browser verifier lets you recompute the fingerprints yourself, and the Status page shows each proof with its own freshness so a stale receipt reads as stale.

Handoff and closeout

Closeout is the last step of a session: the moment the work stops and the records are updated. Handoff is the document that closeout produces. It states what was built, what is mid-flight, and the exact next step, so the next session can pick up days or weeks later without re-learning the project. When the Roadmap describes "handoff discipline", this is what it means: the roadmap page is itself maintained by that process.

Built for humans and AI agents

The same record is published in machine-readable form so that AI (artificial intelligence) agents and search assistants can cite it accurately instead of guessing. /agents.json describes the site's public endpoints; /.well-known/llms.txt is the plain-text index written for language models, pointing at a per-project fact sheet under each project's .ai/ path. If you are reading this through an assistant, that index is the source it should be quoting.

Glossary

Session
One numbered block of work (S1, S2, …) on a single project, opened with a brief and closed with a closeout.
SIL score
Studio Intelligence Ledger score: the studio's self-audit of a session across ten categories, out of 1000.
Canon
The studio's numbered, studio-wide rules (CANON-001 onward) that every project is held to.
Receipt
A published record binding a claim to the fingerprint of the exact build it was checked against.
Closeout
The final step of a session, when outcomes are written back to the project's records.
Handoff
The document a closeout produces: what shipped, what is mid-flight, and the next step.
Studio OS
Studio Operating System: the shared set of records, rules, and rituals above that every VaultSpark project runs on.
IGNIS
The studio's analytics and intelligence engine; it publishes the SIL score and the signals behind the Oracle.
Obelisk
The studio's identity and trust layer, being built to give one account across every VaultSpark game, tool, and surface.

These are the studio's own working terms, and this page will grow as the vocabulary does. Spotted a term that is not explained here? Tell us at vaultsparkstudios.com/contact.
